# TOPIC: Work, Economy & Social Equality Topics

## 1. PERSONA POOL

- [P1] **Isabelle Roy, Workplace Equality Researcher**: Data-driven and fair-minded; discusses gender gaps in senior positions, mentorship, and institutional reform.
- [P2] **Marcus Chen, Career & HR Consultant**: Practical and encouraging; discusses career progression, remote/team-based work, and how technology reshapes jobs.
- [P3] **Helen Osei, Economist**: Calm and explanatory; breaks down concepts like inflation, currency, and economic inequality using everyday examples.
- [P4] **IELTS Examiner**: Professional, neutral examiner assessing fluency, vocabulary range, and grammatical accuracy; asks structured follow-up questions and gives no opinions of their own.

## 2. QUESTION POOL

### Band 5.0 - 6.0
- Q_5_01: Do you think it's normal for both parents to work in a family nowadays?
- Q_5_02: What kind of job would you like to have in the future?
- Q_5_03: Do you think men and women have equal chances of getting promoted at work?
- Q_5_04: Do you prefer working alone or in a team? Why?
- Q_5_05: Do you think technology has made it easier or harder to find a good job?
- Q_5_06: What do you think causes prices to go up in your country?

### Band 6.5+
- Q_7_01: Why do you think women remain underrepresented in senior academic and corporate positions, even where discrimination laws exist?
- Q_7_02: Some people believe career paths are becoming less predictable, involving more "horizontal" moves rather than steady promotion. Do you agree, and what does this mean for young workers?
- Q_7_03: To what extent is rising income inequality caused by technology rather than unfair business practices?
- Q_7_04: How has the rising cost of housing and education changed family financial decisions and the number of dual-income households?
- Q_7_05: What are the benefits and drawbacks of team-based work compared to traditional individual roles in an organisation?
- Q_7_06: Should governments intervene to reduce economic inequality caused by technological change, or is this an inevitable side effect of progress?

## 3. VOCABULARY & COLLOCATION POOL

### Band 5.0 - 6.0
- **glass ceiling**: an invisible barrier that stops women or minorities from being promoted to top positions
- **equal opportunity legislation**: laws designed to prevent discrimination in the workplace
- **supportive mentor**: an experienced person who helps guide someone's career
- **middle-class family**: a family with a moderate income, neither rich nor poor
- **dual-income household**: a family where both partners earn a salary
- **vertical movement (career)**: being promoted to a higher position within an organisation
- **horizontal movement (career)**: moving into a different job function at the same level
- **unit of account**: a standard measure used to express the value of goods and services (i.e. money)
- **inflation rate**: the rate at which prices generally rise over time
- **team-based learning/work**: an approach where tasks are completed collaboratively rather than individually

### Band 6.5+
- **the upper echelons of (a field)**: the highest, most senior levels of an organisation or profession
- **to be under-represented**: to have unfairly low numbers of a group present in a field
- **winner-take-all effect**: a situation where a small number of top performers capture most of the rewards, leaving little for others
- **organisational hierarchy**: the ranked structure of positions within a company or institution
- **radial movement (career)**: a shift toward or away from an organisation's "inner circle" of power, not always tied to promotion
- **retraining**: learning new skills in order to change job function or field
- **gold standard**: a monetary system where currency value is fixed to a set amount of gold
- **fiat currency**: money whose value is not backed by a physical commodity, but by government decree
- **purchasing power**: the amount of goods or services that a unit of money can buy
- **sound money**: currency whose value remains stable and does not decline in purchasing power over time
- **stop-go (inconsistency)**: a pattern of starting and stopping progress, leading to inefficiency
- **to command enormous incomes**: to be able to charge or earn very high pay due to rare skill or demand

## 4. GRAMMAR & RESPONSE PATTERNS

- Pattern_1 (Statistic + concession): "[Only + number] of the [total] are [X], while [comparison in another group]." → e.g. "Only 15 of the 385 professors are women, while the number is even lower in physics."
- Pattern_2 (Cause attribution): "This [trend] appears to be due, in large part, to [cause], although [nuance]." → e.g. "This gap appears to be due, in large part, to a lack of mentorship, although attitudes are slowly changing."
- Pattern_3 (Rhetorical pivot): "Why is this? [Explanation follows]." — used to move from stating a fact to analysing its cause.
- Pattern_4 (Hedged prediction): "It is likely/unclear whether [future outcome], given [current trend]." → e.g. "It is unclear whether lost jobs will be replaced by others that are equally well-paid."
- Pattern_5 (Cyclical process description): "[Subject] develop(s) through repeated cycles of [stage A] and [stage B]." → e.g. "Careers develop through repeated cycles of stability and change."
- Pattern_6 (Historical cause-effect chain): "The introduction of [policy/event] in [year] meant that [consequence]." → e.g. "The introduction of the Bretton-Woods Agreement in 1946 meant that currencies were fixed to the US dollar."
- Pattern_7 (Balanced evaluation): "[Trend] is, while helpful, [limitation] — because [deeper cause]." → e.g. "Stricter regulation is, while helpful, not enough to fix the problem — because the root cause is structural."

## 3. VOCABULARY & COLLOCATION POOL
### Band 5.0 - 6.0
- **arts subjects**: subjects such as history, literature, or languages in contrast to scientific subjects.
- **qualifications**: the examinations that you have passed after completing a course.
- **tuition fees**: the amount of money that you pay to a university for your education.
- **lectures**: talks that someone gives in order to teach people about a particular subject, usually at a university or college.
- **seminars**: classes at a college or university in which the teacher and a small group of students discuss a topic.
- **fail an exam**: be unsuccessful in an exam.
- **retake an exam**: take an exam again because you did not pass it the first time.
- **pass an exam**: be of an acceptable standard in an exam.
- **coursework**: work done by a student during their studies that forms part of their final grade or mark.
- **dream job**: perfect job.
- **job security**: a feeling of being safe and free from worry because your job is permanent.
- **earn a wage**: receive money for work.
- **job satisfaction**: the feeling of pleasure and achievement that you experience in your job when you know that your work is worth doing.
- **part-time**: working less than full-time hours.

### Band 6.5+
- **an office job**: working in an office environment, often associated with routine.
- **long hours**: working for a long time each day.
- **redundant**: having lost your job because your employer no longer needs you.
- **sacked**: dismissed from employment.
- **own boss**: working for oneself; self-employed.
- **breadwinner**: the member of a family who earns the money that the family needs.
- **career-minded**: focused on advancing in one's profession.
- **responsible for**: having the duty of taking care of or managing something.
- **workaholic**: a person who compulsively works hard and long hours.
- **overtime**: time in addition to what is normal, as time worked beyond one's scheduled working hours.
- **volunteer work**: work done for free, often for a charity or community organization.
- **stay-at-home mum/dad**: a parent who stays at home to raise children and manage the household instead of working outside the home.
- **unemployment**: the state of being unemployed.
- **presentation**: a speech or talk in which a new product, idea, or piece of work is shown and explained to an audience.
- **promotion**: the action of raising someone to a higher position or rank.
- **appraisals**: a formal assessment, typically in an interview, of the performance of an employee over a particular period.
- **lay off**: discharge a worker temporarily or permanently because of a shortage of work.
- **stand out**: to be very easy to see or notice; to be much better than other similar things or people.
- **escapism**: the tendency to seek distraction and relief from unpleasant realities, especially by seeking entertainment or engaging in fantasy.
- **intrinsic motivation**: engaging in a behavior because it is personally rewarding; essentially, performing an activity for its own sake rather than the desire for some external reward.

## 4. GRAMMAR & RESPONSE PATTERNS

- Pattern_5: "If I study harder, I will get my dream job." (First conditional for real possibilities)
- Pattern_6: "If I studied harder, I would get my dream job." (Second conditional for unreal possibilities in the present/future)

- Pattern_9: "However, it's fair to say that a university degree is not everything and it's what you do with it that matters." (Using 'However, it's fair to say that' to introduce a balanced perspective)
- Pattern_10: "I'd hazard a guess that youth workers, for example, find it very rewarding helping a young person to achieve their personal goals..." (Using 'I'd hazard a guess that' for cautious speculation)

# TOPIC: Academic Discussion — Presentation Planning & Professional History

## 1. PERSONA POOL

- [P1] **Prof. Diane Marsh, Sociology Tutor**: Encouraging and inquisitive; helps a student narrow down a presentation topic by asking guiding questions and offering suggestions.
- [P2] **University Student (Presentation Preparation)**: Thoughtful but still developing ideas; explains topic choices, reasons, and areas still undecided.
- [P3] **Medical History Commentator**: Neutral, fact-based; explains how professional licensing and regulation systems (e.g. for doctors) developed over time.
- [P4] **IELTS Examiner**: Professional, neutral examiner assessing fluency, vocabulary range, and grammatical accuracy; asks structured follow-up questions and gives no opinions of their own.

## 2. QUESTION POOL

### Band 5.0 - 6.0
- Q_5_01: Have you ever had to prepare a presentation for school or work? How did you choose your topic?
- Q_5_02: Do you prefer working with a supervisor/tutor who gives a lot of guidance, or one who lets you decide everything yourself?
- Q_5_03: Do you think it's important for professions (like doctors) to be officially licensed?
- Q_5_04: What's a profession you find interesting to learn about, and why?
- Q_5_05: Do you usually do research in a library or online?
- Q_5_06: Have you ever changed your topic or plan partway through a project?

### Band 6.5+
- Q_7_01: The student in this conversation chose his topic partly because his professor suggested it and partly because information was easy to find. Do you think convenience should influence academic topic choices, or should students always pick the most challenging or original topic?
- Q_7_02: Why do you think professions like medicine became increasingly regulated and licensed over time? What are the risks of an unregulated profession?
- Q_7_03: How useful is it to trace the "historical development" of a profession (e.g. from unregulated to highly structured) when trying to understand it today?
- Q_7_04: To what extent should students rely on their tutor's suggestions when narrowing down a research or presentation topic?
- Q_7_05: What are the advantages of a public registry (e.g. of licensed doctors) for a society, and are there any drawbacks?
- Q_7_06: Compare the value of studying a topic through historical development versus studying its current state only. Which approach gives a deeper understanding, in your opinion?

## 3. VOCABULARY & COLLOCATION POOL

### Band 5.0 - 6.0
- **presentation topic**: the subject chosen for a talk or academic presentation
- **to do research**: to investigate a topic in detail, often using books or the internet
- **textbook**: a book used for studying a particular subject
- **profession**: a paid occupation, especially one requiring specialised training
- **to focus on (an aspect)**: to give particular attention to one part of a topic
- **training**: the process of learning the skills needed for a job
- **licence / to be licensed**: an official permit to practise a profession legally
- **to improve over time**: to become better gradually across a period
- **medical school**: an institution where people train to become doctors
- **experienced**: having a lot of knowledge or skill gained from doing something for a long time

### Band 6.5+
- **to become more regulated over time**: a field gradually gaining more official rules and oversight
- **to claim to be (a professional)**: to state, possibly falsely, that one holds a certain professional status
- **to establish (an institution)**: to officially create or found an organisation
- **initial function**: the original purpose an organisation had when it was created
- **to issue (licences/certificates) to (qualified people)**: to formally grant official documents to those who meet requirements
- **influential (organisation)**: having a strong effect on decisions, policy, or public opinion
- **government policy**: official plans and actions of a government on a particular issue
- **national registry**: an official, government-maintained public record (e.g. of licensed professionals)
- **career-grade system**: a structured system that ranks professionals by experience level
- **to gain experience and develop skills**: to build practical knowledge/ability through hands-on work, typically under supervision

## 4. GRAMMAR & RESPONSE PATTERNS

- Pattern_1 (Explaining multiple reasons): "Well, a couple of reasons, actually... First, [reason 1]. The other reason is that [reason 2]." — a clear structure for justifying a choice with multiple points.
- Pattern_2 (Tutor scaffolding with a follow-up question): "That's convenient. [Positive consequence]. Um, have you decided which [aspect] you want to focus on?" — guiding a student from a general statement to a more specific plan.
- Pattern_3 (Reporting a historical change succinctly): "Before [time period], there was no formal system to [action]. But in [year], [institution] was established." — a compact way to summarise historical development.
- Pattern_4 (Describing institutional function): "Its initial function was to [purpose 1] and [purpose 2]." → e.g. "Its initial function was to conduct exams and issue medical licenses."
- Pattern_5 (Showing cause and resulting influence): "As a result, it has had a great deal of influence over [area], / for example, it played a major role in [specific event]."
- Pattern_6 (Requesting clarification politely, mid-discussion): "What do you mean?" / "Is that someone who [clarifying guess]?" — natural ways to check understanding in an academic conversation.
- Pattern_7 (Summarising progress + redirecting to next step): "So... it sounds like you've found a lot of useful information about [X]. Um, for the second part of [Y], I want to talk about [Z]. But I'm not really sure what to include."
