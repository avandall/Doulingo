# TOPIC: Tourism, Parks & Leisure Activities

## 1. PERSONA POOL

- [P1] **Park/Festival Information Officer**: Friendly and precise; answers phone questions about opening times, admission fees, and facilities at a park or festival.
- [P2] **Visitor Centre Guide (Outdoor Activities)**: Enthusiastic and organised; briefs visitors on cycling routes, bike rental, safety equipment, and food options before an outdoor activity.
- [P3] **Adventure Tour Guide (Scuba/Boat Tour)**: Energetic but safety-conscious; welcomes a group, introduces themselves, and runs through safety precautions before an activity begins.
- [P4] **IELTS Examiner**: Professional, neutral examiner assessing fluency, vocabulary range, and grammatical accuracy; asks structured follow-up questions and gives no opinions of their own.

## 2. QUESTION POOL

### Band 5.0 - 6.0
- Q_5_01: Do you enjoy visiting parks or festivals in your free time?
- Q_5_02: Have you ever rented a bicycle or other equipment for an outdoor activity?
- Q_5_03: What information do you usually check before visiting a new place (opening hours, prices, etc.)?
- Q_5_04: Do you prefer guided tours or exploring a place by yourself? Why?
- Q_5_05: Have you ever done an adventure activity, like diving, hiking, or boating?
- Q_5_06: Do you think children and elderly people should get discounts on admission fees?

### Band 6.5+
- Q_7_01: Why do you think many tourist attractions offer free admission for children and the elderly, but not for other groups?
- Q_7_02: What responsibilities do tour guides have when leading inexperienced participants (like new divers) through a potentially risky activity?
- Q_7_03: To what extent do visitor centres and information booths improve the overall experience of visiting a natural area?
- Q_7_04: Compare the benefits of renting equipment (like bicycles) versus bringing your own when travelling. Which do you think is more practical, and why?
- Q_7_05: Do you think safety briefings (like the ones given before scuba diving) are usually taken seriously by tourists? Why or why not?
- Q_7_06: How do coupons and discount partnerships (e.g. restaurant coupons from a visitor centre) affect the local businesses around a tourist attraction?

## 3. VOCABULARY & COLLOCATION POOL

### Band 5.0 - 6.0
- **admission fee**: the price charged to enter a place (park, museum, festival)
- **free parking**: parking available at no cost
- **opening hours**: the times during which a place is open to the public
- **elderly people**: older people, often referred to respectfully in this way
- **scenic course/route**: a route notable for beautiful views
- **safety equipment**: gear designed to protect someone (e.g. helmet, elbow pads)
- **to rent (something) at a reasonable rate**: to hire an item for a fair, affordable price
- **dining area**: a designated space where food is eaten
- **guided tour**: a tour led by a person who explains information about the place
- **coupon**: a printed or digital voucher offering a discount

### Band 6.5+
- **to be voted Best [Category] in annual surveys**: to receive a top ranking through a yearly public poll
- **safety precaution**: an action taken in advance to prevent harm or accidents
- **inexperienced participants**: people taking part in an activity who have little or no prior experience
- **to pay close attention**: to concentrate carefully and fully on something (used when warning about safety)
- **eatery**: an informal term for a restaurant or place to eat
- **to import (plants/materials) from (a region)**: to bring something in from another country or area
- **citywide project**: an initiative or plan that covers an entire city
- **to be right next to / just across the road from**: precise spatial expressions used for giving directions or describing location
- **to board (a boat/bus)**: to get onto a vehicle or vessel at the start of a journey
- **a couple of minutes' walk**: an informal way of expressing a very short walking distance

## 4. GRAMMAR & RESPONSE PATTERNS

- Pattern_1 (Giving directions with sequence): "Go straight ahead from [starting point], then turn [left/right] onto [path]. You'll see [landmark] on your [left/right]." — a full spatial-directions structure.
- Pattern_2 (Comparative eligibility for pricing): "It's [price] for [group A], / [students/over a certain age] only pay [price], / [group B] don't have to pay at all." → e.g. "It's £5 for adults; students 13 and older only pay £3; children and elderly people don't have to pay."
- Pattern_3 (Self-introduction + plan announcement): "My name is [Name], and I'll be your guide today. In a few minutes, we'll be [action]." — a standard tour-guide opening structure.
- Pattern_4 (Reassuring transition before instructions): "But first, I'd like to go over [safety information/instructions]. A lot of you are [description], so please [request]."
- Pattern_5 (Listing available options/facilities): "As for [category], there is a wide variety of [options] to choose from for [purpose]." → e.g. "As for food, there is a wide variety of restaurants to choose from for lunch or dinner."
- Pattern_6 (Offering help beyond the current request): "They can also [additional service], such as [example]." → e.g. "They can also give you coupons for some of the most popular eateries."
- Pattern_7 (Polite closing offer): "No problem. Feel free to call again." / "OK, I'll call if I need anything else." — standard closing exchange for a service phone call.

# TOPIC: News Sources

## 1. PERSONA POOL
- [P1] **The Traditionalist**: Prefers getting news from local newspapers and traditional radio stations. Values community news, in-depth analysis, and finds modern media too sensational or full of advertising.
- [P2] **The Digital Native**: Relies primarily on the Internet, social media, and online news portals. Values the immediacy, variety, and interactive nature of online news but acknowledges the issue of fake news and information overload.
- [P3] **The Skeptic**: Critically evaluates news sources. Is aware of media bias, censorship, and the difference between hard-hitting journalism and mere entertainment or "cheesy" stories.
- [P4] **IELTS Examiner**: Professional, neutral examiner assessing fluency, vocabulary, and grammar.

## 2. QUESTION POOL
### Band 5.0 - 6.0
- Q_5_01: Where do you get the best news and information about your family?
- Q_5_02: Where do you get news about your country or region and the world?
- Q_5_03: Have you ever read a foreign newspaper?
- Q_5_04: Have you ever met a famous person?
- Q_5_05: Describe your favourite magazine or newspaper.

### Band 6.5+
- Q_7_01: Describe your favourite news source.
- Q_7_02: How often do you use this news source?
- Q_7_03: Why do you use this source and what other sources do you use?
- Q_7_04: How would you change that news source if you could?
- Q_7_05: What are the advantages and disadvantages of getting news from the Internet compared to traditional newspapers?
- Q_7_06: Do you think the news we receive is always accurate and unbiased?

## 3. VOCABULARY & COLLOCATION POOL
### Band 5.0 - 6.0
- **news**: newly received or noteworthy information, especially about recent events.
- **opinion**: a view or judgement formed about something, not necessarily based on fact or knowledge.
- **newspaper**: a printed publication (usually issued daily or weekly) consisting of folded unstapled sheets and containing news, articles, advertisements, and correspondence.
- **magazine**: a periodical publication containing articles and illustrations, often on a particular subject or aimed at a particular readership.
- **the Internet**: a global computer network providing a variety of information and communication facilities.
- **television**: a system for converting visual images (with sound) into electrical signals, transmitting them by radio or other means, and displaying them electronically on a screen.
- **radio**: the transmission and reception of electromagnetic waves of radio frequency, especially those carrying sound messages.
- **local**: relating or restricted to a particular area or one's neighbourhood.
- **international**: existing, occurring, or carried on between nations.
- **funny**: causing laughter or amusement; humorous.
- **boring**: not interesting; tedious.

### Band 6.5+
- **ban**: officially or legally prohibit (something).
- **deception**: the action of deceiving someone.
- **disclosure**: the making known of secret or new information.
- **discretion**: the freedom to decide what should be done in a particular situation.
- **moral**: concerned with the principles of right and wrong behaviour.
- **principle**: a fundamental truth or proposition that serves as the foundation for a system of belief or behaviour.
- **regulation**: a rule or directive made and maintained by an authority.
- **condemned**: express complete disapproval of; censure.
- **controversial**: giving rise or likely to give rise to controversy or public disagreement.
- **critical**: expressing adverse or disapproving comments or judgements.
- **devious**: showing a skilful use of underhand tactics to achieve goals.
- **discerning/discriminating**: having or showing good judgement.
- **hard-hitting**: uncompromisingly direct and honest.
- **provocative**: causing anger or another strong reaction, especially deliberately.
- **thought-provoking**: stimulating careful consideration or attention.
- **a regulatory body**: a public authority or government agency responsible for exercising autonomous authority over some area of human activity in a regulatory or supervisory capacity.
- **in confidence**: as a secret.
- **to conceal/suppress/withhold information**: to keep information secret or prevent it from being known.
- **to filter/screen**: to process or assess information to remove unwanted elements.
- **to keep under wraps**: to keep something secret.

## 4. GRAMMAR & RESPONSE PATTERNS
- Pattern_1: "Have you ever read a newspaper from start to finish? Yes, I have." (Using the Present Perfect to talk about experience up to now)
- Pattern_2: "I bought my first magazine when I was a teenager." (Using Past Simple when specifying *when* something happened)
- Pattern_3: "I've lived there for 5 years since my family moved from the countryside." (Present Perfect for uncompleted actions continuing to the present)
- Pattern_4: "Newspapers have changed. There is much more international news now." (Present Perfect for describing the present result of a past change)
- Pattern_5: "The TV programmes contained bad language and violence, so our parents censored them." (Using 'so' to link a reason to a result)
- Pattern_6: "Our parents censored the TV programmes because of the bad language and violence." (Using 'because/because of' to link a result to a reason)
- Pattern_7: "My favourite source of news is the student radio station in Auckland... because the radio is in my car." (Clearly stating a preference and providing a practical reason)
- Pattern_8: "The reports of events are often quite well analysed, from many different points of view, so they are a good source of news and entertainment." (Using 'so' for a logical conclusion based on a prior statement)
- Pattern_9: "I used to listen to the National Radio station for news, and they also have intelligent commentaries, but not as good music!" (Comparing past habits with present preferences using 'used to' and 'but')
- Pattern_10: "I would like the paper to have less advertising, however. It makes it too hard to find the interesting parts!" (Expressing a desire for change and justifying it)

# TOPIC: Leisure Activities

## 1. PERSONA POOL
- [P1] **The Active Outdoorsperson**: Loves spending free time outside, engaging in activities like gardening, countryside walks, or sports. Values fresh air, exercise, and the restorative power of nature.
- [P2] **The Indoor Hobbyist**: Prefers relaxing or mentally stimulating indoor activities like reading, playing board games, embroidery, or watching movies. Enjoys quiet time or intimate gatherings.
- [P3] **The Social Butterfly**: Free time is all about connecting with others. Enjoys going to cafes, chatting online, using social media (Facebook, Twitter), and meeting friends for entertainment.
- [P4] **IELTS Examiner**: Professional, neutral examiner assessing fluency, vocabulary, and grammar.

## 2. QUESTION POOL
### Band 5.0 - 6.0
- Q_5_01: What do you like doing in your spare time?
- Q_5_02: Do you prefer outdoor or indoor pastimes?
- Q_5_03: What other leisure activities do you enjoy which are not in the usual lists?
- Q_5_04: Do you prefer spending your leisure time alone or with friends?
- Q_5_05: How do you keep in touch with your friends?

### Band 6.5+
- Q_7_01: Describe your favourite leisure activity (what it is, when & where you do it, who with, and why you enjoy it).
- Q_7_02: Why do you think the older generation enjoy different leisure activities to the younger generation?
- Q_7_03: In your country, how do people's leisure activities today compare to those in the past?
- Q_7_04: How do you think leisure activities might change in the future?
- Q_7_05: Is it important to have leisure activities that are completely different from your job or studies?

## 3. VOCABULARY & COLLOCATION POOL
### Band 5.0 - 6.0
- **spare time / free time**: time available for hobbies and other activities that you enjoy.
- **hobby / pastime**: an activity done regularly in one's leisure time for pleasure.
- **relaxing**: reducing tension or anxiety.
- **popular**: liked or admired by many people.
- **energetic**: showing or involving great activity or vitality.
- **fishing**: the activity of catching fish, either for food or as a sport.
- **gardening**: the activity of tending and cultivating a garden, especially as a pastime.
- **shopping**: the purchasing of goods from stores.
- **listening to music**: paying attention to musical sounds.
- **movies**: cinema films.
- **for fun / for enjoyment**: for pleasure.
- **to relax**: to rest and become less anxious.

### Band 6.5+
- **beneficial**: resulting in good; favorable or advantageous.
- **challenging**: testing one's abilities; demanding.
- **enthralling**: capturing and holding one's attention; fascinating.
- **fascinating**: extremely interesting.
- **intriguing**: arousing one's curiosity or interest; fascinating.
- **monotonous**: dull, tedious, and repetitious; lacking in variety and interest.
- **satisfying**: giving fulfillment or the pleasure associated with this.
- **tranquil**: free from disturbance; calm.
- **bird-watching**: the observation of birds in their natural habitat as a hobby.
- **star-gazing**: observing the stars.
- **embroidery**: the art or pastime of embroidering cloth.
- **patchwork quilting**: the craft of sewing small pieces of cloth in different designs, colors, or textures together to make a larger piece.
- **scrapbooking**: the activity of creating blank books containing photographs, newspaper cuttings, and other memorabilia.
- **to socialize**: to mix socially with others.
- **to keep in touch**: to maintain communications with someone.

## 4. GRAMMAR & RESPONSE PATTERNS
- Pattern_1: "I like to chat with my friends online for fun." (Using adverbial phrases of purpose: 'for + noun')
- Pattern_2: "I also love going to the movies to relax." (Using adverbial phrases of purpose: 'to + infinitive verb')
- Pattern_3: "I use Facebook to keep in touch with my friends." (Using 'to + infinitive' to explain purpose)
- Pattern_4: "People used to play cards. However, nowadays they prefer to play video games." (Using 'However' to contrast past and present habits)
- Pattern_5: "In the past, children played outside more often, whereas nowadays they spend more time indoors." (Using 'whereas' for contrast)
- Pattern_6: "Older people might enjoy gardening, while younger people might prefer clubbing." (Using 'while' for contrast)
- Pattern_7: "In my opinion, the main reason for this change is the development of technology." (Using 'In my opinion' to introduce a viewpoint)
- Pattern_8: "It seems to me that people have less free time now than they used to." (Using 'It seems to me that' for a softer statement of opinion)
- Pattern_9: "I think one of the reasons is that life has become much faster-paced." (Structuring an argument by listing reasons)
- Pattern_10: "I probably use it almost every day, I would say I'm getting addicted to it!" (Using conversational fillers 'I would say' and expressing a strong habit)
