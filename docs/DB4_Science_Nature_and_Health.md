# TOPIC: Nature and environment

## 1. PERSONA POOL
- [P1] **Nature Lover**: Enjoys spending time outdoors, appreciates natural beauty, and finds peace in quiet, remote locations. Prefers the countryside over the city.
- [P2] **Urbanite**: Prefers the convenience and excitement of city life. Finds rural areas boring or lonely and dislikes being away from amenities.
- [P3] **Concerned Citizen**: Worries about the environmental impact of human activities, such as pollution and the introduction of invasive species. Supports conservation efforts.
- [P4] **IELTS Examiner**: Professional, neutral examiner assessing fluency, vocabulary, and grammar.

## 2. QUESTION POOL
### Band 4.5 - 6.0
- Q_5_01: Do you find places like the scene in the picture beautiful or boring?
- Q_5_02: Why are places like this attractive to people?
- Q_5_03: Why do we need places like this more and more in the modern world?
- Q_5_04: What kind of places do you like to visit? Do you prefer places in towns and cities or in the countryside?
- Q_5_05: Describe an animal which belonged to you or someone you know.
- Q_5_06: Describe a present you received and that you liked very much.

## 3. VOCABULARY & COLLOCATION POOL
### Band 4.5 - 6.0
- **peaceful**: free from disturbance; tranquil.
- **remote**: (of a place) situated far from the main centres of population; distant.
- **bearable**: able to be endured.
- **noisy**: making or given to making a lot of noise.
- **relaxing**: reducing tension or anxiety.
- **restful**: having a quiet and soothing quality.
- **welcoming**: behaving in a polite or friendly way to a guest or new arrival.
- **appealing**: attractive or interesting.
- **pet**: a domestic or tamed animal kept for companionship or pleasure.
- **lively**: full of life and energy; active and outgoing.
- **wagging**: (especially with reference to an animal's tail) move or cause to move rapidly to and fro.
- **bark**: the sharp explosive cry of a dog, fox, or seal.
- **purr**: (of a cat) make a low continuous vibratory sound expressing contentment.
- **stroke**: move one's hand with gentle pressure over (a surface), typically repeatedly; caress.
- **groom**: brush and clean the coat of (a horse, dog, or other animal).
- **gift/present**: a thing given willingly to someone without payment; a present.
- **sentimental value**: importance to someone because of a connection with a happy time of life, a special person, etc.

## 4. GRAMMAR & RESPONSE PATTERNS
- Pattern_1: "What makes the place so attractive is the open fields." (Using a 'What' pseudo-cleft sentence for emphasis)
- Pattern_2: "What I like most about the building is the large windows." (Using a 'What' clause to highlight a specific feature)
- Pattern_3: "The building that I like most is..." (Using a defining relative clause to introduce a topic)
- Pattern_4: "Actually, you're right. It might be better to wait until another day." (Changing an opinion politely in a discussion)
- Pattern_5: "Yes, that's what I meant, in the evening." (Clarifying a point after changing one's mind)
- Pattern_6: "The fondest memory I have is of taking him for walks..." (Introducing a memory)
- Pattern_7: "But what I remember most is letting her curl up on my lap..." (Emphasizing a specific memory)
- Pattern_8: "What sticks in my mind is the way she used to get excited..." (Using a colloquial expression to introduce a strong memory)
- Pattern_9: "We wanted an active dog, but we got a lazy dog." (Using contrastive stress in speech to highlight opposite ideas)

# TOPIC: Health and fitness

## 1. PERSONA POOL
- [P1] **Sports Enthusiast**: Loves playing and watching various sports. Believes physical activity is crucial for physical and mental health. Enjoys the social and competitive aspects of sports.
- [P2] **Health-Conscious Individual**: Focuses on a balanced lifestyle. Might not be a hardcore athlete but takes steps to stay healthy, such as eating well, drinking water, and managing stress.
- [P3] **Reluctant Exerciser**: Knows they should be healthier but finds it difficult to maintain a routine. Might complain about minor health issues or the difficulty of avoiding convenience foods.
- [P4] **IELTS Examiner**: Professional, neutral examiner assessing fluency, vocabulary, and grammar.

## 2. QUESTION POOL
### Band 4.5 - 6.0
- Q_5_01: What is your favourite sport?
- Q_5_02: Which areas of sport appeal to you?
- Q_5_03: Which areas of sport would you avoid?
- Q_5_04: What do you think is the link between sport and a healthy population?
- Q_5_05: What do you think is the cause of the increase in people doing sport?
- Q_5_06: How important is the social aspect of participating in sport?

## 3. VOCABULARY & COLLOCATION POOL
### Band 4.5 - 6.0
- **water sports**: sports played on or in water.
- **adventure sports**: sports that involve a high degree of risk or physical exertion.
- **motor sports**: sporting events involving the racing of motorized vehicles.
- **team sports**: sports played by teams.
- **non-contact sports**: sports where players are separated or physical contact is penalized.
- **racket sports**: games in which players use rackets to hit a ball or other object.
- **indoor sports**: sports played inside a building.
- **outdoor sports**: sports played outside.
- **physical exercise**: bodily activity that enhances or maintains physical fitness.
- **spectator sports**: a sport that many people find entertaining to watch.
- **mentally alert**: quick to notice any unusual and potentially dangerous or difficult circumstances.
- **illnesses**: a disease or period of sickness affecting the body or mind.
- **competitive atmosphere**: an environment where people are trying to win or be better than others.
- **convenience foods**: food that is almost ready to eat when it is bought and can be prepared quickly and easily.
- **moderate exercise**: physical activity that requires a moderate amount of effort and noticeably accelerates the heart rate.

## 4. GRAMMAR & RESPONSE PATTERNS
- Pattern_1: "I prefer contact sports like football and rugby, mainly because they're a good way to get rid of my aggression." (Stating a preference and giving a reason)
- Pattern_2: "Swimming is the only individual sport I like, as I usually prefer being with other people." (Expressing a specific preference and contrasting it with a general rule)
- Pattern_3: "Doing sport makes people more mentally alert." (Making a general, abstract statement from a personal observation)
- Pattern_4: "Since I've been doing more exercise, I get fewer illnesses." (Using 'since' to show cause and effect)
- Pattern_5: "It's mostly because of the way the creature looks and they can also be venomous." (Using 'It's mostly because of' to emphasize a main point)
- Pattern_6: "I suppose the best way is to maintain a balanced and healthy diet..." (Using 'I suppose the best way is' to offer a considered opinion)
- Pattern_7: "I'd say... is the key thing." (Emphasizing a crucial factor)
- Pattern_8: "It's obvious that..." (Stating something clearly evident)
- Pattern_9: "I'm not sure really. Most research is beneficial but some can be unethical..." (Taking time to think and offering a balanced view when unsure)

# TOPIC: World issues and environment

## 1. PERSONA POOL
- [P1] **Environmental Activist**: Deeply concerned about global warming, deforestation, and fossil fuel emissions. Actively campaigns for change and believes both individuals and governments must do more.
- [P2] **Global Economist**: Focuses on the humanitarian and economic impacts of disasters. Discusses famine, poverty, and unemployment resulting from extreme weather and conflict.
- [P3] **Pragmatic Citizen**: Takes small personal steps to help the environment (recycling, turning off lights) but feels that major corporations and governments hold the real power to effect change.
- [P4] **IELTS Examiner**: Professional, neutral examiner assessing fluency, vocabulary, and grammar.

## 2. QUESTION POOL
### Band 5.0 - 6.0
- Q_5_01: What's the climate like in your country?
- Q_5_02: Does it often snow in your country?
- Q_5_03: Describe something you do to help the environment.

### Band 6.5+
- Q_7_01: How has the climate changed in recent years?
- Q_7_02: What can individuals do to protect the environment?
- Q_7_03: Do you believe businesses do enough to protect the environment?
- Q_7_04: How will climate change affect people's lives in the future?
- Q_7_05: What are some of the causes of water pollution?
- Q_7_06: What consequences can a shortage of rain have on people's lives?
- Q_7_07: What are the differences between old and young people's attitudes towards protecting the environment?

## 3. VOCABULARY & COLLOCATION POOL
### Band 5.0 - 6.0
- **deluge**: a severe flood.
- **heat wave**: a prolonged period of abnormally hot weather.
- **hurricane**: a storm with a violent wind, in particular a tropical cyclone in the Caribbean.
- **earthquake**: a sudden and violent shaking of the ground.
- **flooding**: the covering or submerging of normally dry land with a large amount of water.
- **recycle**: convert waste into reusable material.
- **reduce**: make smaller or less in amount, degree, or size.
- **save**: keep safe or rescue (someone or something) from harm or danger.
- **waste**: use or expend carelessly, extravagantly, or to no purpose.

### Band 6.5+
- **devastation**: great destruction or damage.
- **civil war**: a war between citizens of the same country.
- **drought**: a prolonged period of abnormally low rainfall, leading to a shortage of water.
- **famine**: extreme scarcity of food.
- **volcanic eruption**: the sudden occurrence of a violent discharge of steam and volcanic material.
- **climate change**: a change in global or regional climate patterns.
- **deforestation**: the action of clearing a wide area of trees.
- **epidemic**: a widespread occurrence of an infectious disease in a community at a particular time.
- **fossil fuel emissions**: the release of greenhouse gases into the atmosphere from burning fossil fuels.
- **global warming**: a gradual increase in the overall temperature of the earth's atmosphere.
- **homelessness**: the state of having no home.
- **migration**: movement from one part of something to another.
- **poverty**: the state of being extremely poor.
- **refugees**: a person who has been forced to leave their country in order to escape war, persecution, or natural disaster.
- **tsunami**: a long high sea wave caused by an earthquake, submarine landslide, or other disturbance.
- **irreversible damage**: damage that cannot be undone or altered.
- **humanitarian disaster**: an event or series of events that represents a critical threat to the health, safety, security or wellbeing of a community.
- **impending crisis**: a crisis that is about to happen.
- **far-reaching consequences**: having important and widely applicable effects or implications.
- **carbon footprint**: the total sets of greenhouse gas emissions caused by an organization, event, product or person.

## 4. GRAMMAR & RESPONSE PATTERNS
- Pattern_1: "An earthquake can lead to a tsunami." (Using 'can lead to' to explain cause and effect)

- Pattern_9: "That's why it's so important to educate people, particularly children, so that they grow up with a good understanding of how to protect our planet..." (Using 'That's why' to link a solution to a previously stated problem)

## 3. VOCABULARY & COLLOCATION POOL

### Band 5.0 - 6.0
- **greenhouse effect**: the warming of the Earth's atmosphere caused by trapped gases
- **fossil fuel**: a non-renewable energy source such as coal, oil, or gas
- **energy efficiency**: using less energy to get the same result
- **car-pooling**: sharing one car between several people to commute together
- **single-occupant vehicle**: a car with only one person (the driver) inside
- **exhaust emission**: the gases released by a vehicle's engine
- **industrial safety**: measures taken to protect workers from harm in factories or workplaces
- **fireproofing agent**: a material used to stop or slow the spread of fire
- **tensile strength**: how much pulling force a material can withstand before breaking
- **weathering agent**: natural forces (rain, wind, temperature) that gradually wear down materials

### Band 6.5+
- **temperature gradient**: the rate at which temperature changes with depth or distance
- **meteorological record**: official historical data about weather and climate
- **provocative finding**: a research result that challenges existing assumptions and prompts debate
- **general circulation model (GCM)**: a computer simulation used to predict climate change
- **to isolate (a variable) from a broader trend**: to separate one specific factor from the overall pattern being studied
- **stiff resistance**: strong opposition to a proposed plan or policy
- **irreversible consequence**: an effect or outcome that cannot be reversed once it occurs
- **down-cycled**: recycled into a product of lower quality than the original material
- **biodegradable**: capable of decomposing naturally without harming the environment
- **respiratory tract**: the passage air travels through in the body, from nose to lungs
- **scar tissue**: tissue that forms after damage, which can reduce an organ's normal function
- **generic name (for a substance)**: the general category name used for a group of related materials or chemicals

## 4. GRAMMAR & RESPONSE PATTERNS

- Pattern_1 (Cautious scientific claim): "[Finding] suggests that at least part of [phenomenon] can be [explained/isolated] by [factor]." → e.g. "The data suggests that at least part of the temperature rise can be explained by urban heat."
- Pattern_2 (Comparative statistic): "[A] requires/emits far less [resource] than [B] does, per [unit]." → e.g. "Rapid rail emits far less nitrogen oxide than cars do, per passenger kilometre."
- Pattern_3 (Concession + main point): "While [acknowledge benefit], [it also/however, main limitation]." → e.g. "While recycling saves raw materials, it still requires fossil fuels to process."
- Pattern_4 (Passive prediction): "[Something] is expected/estimated to [verb] by/at [figure]." → e.g. "Recycled fibre use is expected to increase at twice the rate of virgin fibre."
- Pattern_5 (Explaining a mechanism, cause chain): "As [particle/factor] [does something], it [triggers/results in] [consequence]." → e.g. "As particles penetrate deeper, they trigger inflammation in the tissue."
- Pattern_6 (Historical/'once considered' framing): "[Material/idea] was once considered [positive label] before [new understanding] became known." → e.g. "Asbestos was once considered a wonder material before its health risks became known."
- Pattern_7 (Policy recommendation): "[Government/institution] should [restrict/encourage] [behaviour], combining [policy A] with [policy B]." → e.g. "Cities should restrict car use, combining investment in public transport with parking limits."
