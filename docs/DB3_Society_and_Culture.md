# TOPIC: The individual and society

## 1. PERSONA POOL
- [P1] **Social Observer**: Interested in group dynamics, how individuals fit into society, and the balance between conformity and rebellion. Thinks about the impact of larger trends on communities.
- [P2] **Community Member**: Focuses on local issues, social awareness, and how individuals can positively influence their immediate surroundings. Values cooperation.
- [P3] **Global Thinker**: Considers international problems like poverty, environmental issues, and globalization. Believes in large-scale solutions and international partnerships.
- [P4] **IELTS Examiner**: Professional, neutral examiner assessing fluency, vocabulary, and grammar.

## 2. QUESTION POOL
### Band 4.5 - 6.0
- Q_5_01: How would you feel about being part of a crowd like this?
- Q_5_02: Do you think individuals shape society or is it the other way round? Why?
- Q_5_03: In what ways can people have a positive influence on the society they live in?
- Q_5_04: How do you think individuals can best contribute to society?
- Q_5_05: Do you think modern societies are in danger of being destroyed by materialism?
- Q_5_06: What is the impact of modern business developments worldwide on societies in general?
- Q_5_07: Describe a place which has influenced your life.

## 3. VOCABULARY & COLLOCATION POOL
### Band 4.5 - 6.0
- **society**: the aggregate of people living together in a more or less ordered community.
- **initiatives**: an act or strategy intended to resolve a difficulty or improve a situation; a fresh approach to something.
- **social awareness**: the ability to take the perspective of and empathize with others from diverse backgrounds and cultures.
- **individual responsibility**: the idea that human beings choose, instigate, or otherwise cause their own actions.
- **conformist**: a person who conforms to accepted behavior or established practices.
- **rebel**: a person who rises in opposition or armed resistance against an established government or ruler, or who resists convention.
- **poverty**: the state of being extremely poor.
- **materialism**: a tendency to consider material possessions and physical comfort as more important than spiritual values.
- **globalization**: the process by which businesses or other organizations develop international influence or start operating on an international scale.
- **oppressive**: weighing heavily on the mind or spirits; causing depression or discomfort.
- **invigorates**: give strength or energy to.
- **depressed**: (of a person) in a state of general unhappiness or despondency.
- **peaceful**: free from disturbance; tranquil.
- **vibrant**: full of energy and enthusiasm.

## 4. GRAMMAR & RESPONSE PATTERNS
- Pattern_1: "Let's now examine the range of initiatives the government has introduced..." (Using signpost phrases to structure ideas, similar to a lecture format)
- Pattern_2: "Another problem we must examine is the meaning of the word society." (Introducing a new point in a sequence)
- Pattern_3: "To sum up, history can be a huge help in many aspects of our lives." (Concluding a point)
- Pattern_4: "I always wear exactly what I like. I don't care if other people think it's not fashionable." (Expressing a non-conformist attitude)
- Pattern_5: "I normally do what the boss tells me at work. Even if I disagree, I keep my head down." (Expressing a conformist attitude)
- Pattern_6: "There's something about the place that just feels oppressive... I think it might be because of the fluorescent lighting..." (Describing a negative feeling associated with a place and giving a reason)
- Pattern_7: "It's difficult to describe what really invigorates me, just a freshness that feels good." (Describing a positive feeling associated with a place)
- Pattern_8: "I remember it mainly because.../It was memorable because..." (Summing up impressions of a place or experience)

# TOPIC: Transport

## 1. PERSONA POOL
- [P1] **The Commuter**: An individual who relies heavily on public transportation (like trains or subways) for their daily journey to work or school. They prioritize efficiency and comfort but are often frustrated by delays and crowds.
- [P2] **The Cyclist**: Someone who prefers active and environmentally friendly modes of transport like cycling. They value health benefits and avoiding traffic but are concerned about safety and the lack of bike lanes.
- [P3] **The Car Driver**: Relies mostly on their private car for convenience and comfort. They often face issues like traffic jams, finding parking, and the high cost of fuel.
- [P4] **IELTS Examiner**: Professional, neutral examiner assessing fluency, vocabulary, and grammar.

## 2. QUESTION POOL
### Band 5.0 - 6.0
- Q_5_01: What kinds of transport do you use every day when you are in your country?
- Q_5_02: How far do you travel every day?
- Q_5_03: What transport do you use most often? Why?
- Q_5_04: How do you get to school every day?
- Q_5_05: What form of transport do you prefer? Why?
- Q_5_06: Do you live near your school?
- Q_5_07: Do you drive to work?

### Band 6.5+
- Q_7_01: Describe the public transport system in your city.
- Q_7_02: Describe a journey you make every day.
- Q_7_03: What problems are there with transport in your city?
- Q_7_04: How can the government solve the problem of traffic jams at rush hour?
- Q_7_05: What would happen if the government put a tax on old cars?
- Q_7_06: How can cities encourage more people to cycle instead of driving?

## 3. VOCABULARY & COLLOCATION POOL
### Band 5.0 - 6.0
- **public transport**: buses, trains, and other forms of transport that are available to the public.
- **traffic jams**: a line or lines of stationary or very slow-moving traffic, caused by roadworks, an accident, or heavy congestion.
- **rush hour**: a time during each day when traffic is at its heaviest.
- **gasoline/petrol**: a liquid obtained from petroleum, used as fuel in internal combustion engines.
- **journey**: an act of travelling from one place to another.
- **suburb**: an outlying district of a city, especially a residential one.
- **commuters**: a person who travels some distance to work on a regular basis.
- **delay**: a period of time by which something is late or postponed.
- **motorway**: a dual-carriageway road designed for fast traffic.
- **convenient**: fitting in well with a person's needs, activities, and plans.
- **expensive**: costing a lot of money.
- **efficient**: achieving maximum productivity with minimum wasted effort or expense.
- **comfortable**: providing physical ease and relaxation.

### Band 6.5+
- **high-rise parking buildings**: multi-story structures designed specifically for automobile parking.
- **penalty**: a punishment imposed for breaking a law, rule, or contract.
- **cycle lane/bike lane**: a part of a road marked off with painted lines, for use by cyclists.
- **speed limit**: the maximum speed at which a vehicle may legally travel on a particular stretch of road.
- **to break down**: (of a machine or motor vehicle) to suddenly cease to function.
- **cancellation of service**: the action of cancelling a scheduled transport service.
- **congested roads**: roads that are so crowded with traffic as to hinder or prevent freedom of movement.
- **detour / traffic diversion**: a long or roundabout route taken to avoid something or to visit somewhere along the way.
- **erratic driving**: driving that is not even or regular in pattern or movement; unpredictable.
- **hold-up**: a delay.
- **roadworks**: repairs to roads or to pipes or cables under roads.
- **cramped**: uncomfortably small or restricted.
- **draughty**: (of an enclosed space) cold and uncomfortable because of currents of cool air.
- **jam-packed**: extremely crowded or full to capacity.
- **tedious**: too long, slow, or dull; tiresome or monotonous.
- **nerve-racking**: causing stress or anxiety.

## 4. GRAMMAR & RESPONSE PATTERNS
- Pattern_1: "I usually go to work by train because it's convenient, but sometimes I walk for the exercise." (Extending answers using conjunctions 'because' and 'but')
- Pattern_2: "No, I don't have a car, so I walk to work every day." (Using 'so' to show a result)
- Pattern_3: "I use a bicycle, or I walk to school." (Using 'or' to present an alternative)
- Pattern_4: "I walk to the bus stop. Then, I get on the bus." (Using 'then' to show sequence)
- Pattern_5: "If the government puts a tax on old cars, there will be fewer breakdowns and traffic jams." (Using the First Conditional: If + present simple, ... will + base verb to propose solutions to problems)
- Pattern_6: "If the government lowers the speed limit, there will be fewer high-speed accidents." (First Conditional for problem solving)
- Pattern_7: "It takes an hour, but I don't mind the journey because I do all the reading for my university on the train." (Combining multiple conjunctions for a complex, fluent answer)
- Pattern_8: "This is another reason why I need to get to the station early in case I have to get a bus." (Using 'in case' to describe a precaution)

# TOPIC: Travel and transport

## 1. PERSONA POOL
- [P1] **Frustrated Commuter**: Deals with rush hour, congestion, and unreliable public transport daily. Wants more cycle lanes, pedestrian crossings, and subsidized fares.
- [P2] **Intrepid Traveler**: Loves exploring remote, unspoilt places off the beaten track. Dislikes mass tourism, commercialized resorts, and tacky souvenirs.
- [P3] **Eco-Tourist**: Cares deeply about ethical tourism. Prefers to travel by train rather than plane, supports local businesses, and seeks authentic cultural experiences.
- [P4] **IELTS Examiner**: Professional, neutral examiner assessing fluency, vocabulary, and grammar.

- Q_7_06: What are the benefits and drawbacks of living in an area overrun by tourists?

## 3. VOCABULARY & COLLOCATION POOL
### Band 5.0 - 6.0
- **rush hour**: a time during each day when traffic is at its heaviest.
- **congestion**: the state of being congested with traffic.
- **cycle lanes**: a part of a road marked off with painted lines, for use by cyclists.
- **pedestrian crossings**: a specified part of a road where pedestrians have right of way to cross.
- **subsidized**: (of an organization or activity) supported financially.
- **breakdown**: a mechanical failure.
- **collision**: an instance of one moving object or person striking violently against another.
- **park and ride**: a system for reducing urban traffic congestion, in which drivers leave their cars in car parks on the outskirts of a city and travel to the city centre on public transport.
- **long flight/journey**: a trip covering a large distance.
- **package holiday**: a holiday organized by a travel agent, with arrangements for transport, accommodation, etc., made at an inclusive price.
- **tourist destination/attraction**: a place that attracts many tourists.

### Band 6.5+
- **traffic calming measures**: the deliberate slowing of traffic in residential areas, by building road bumps or other obstructions.
- **long-haul flight**: a flight that takes place over a long distance.
- **eco-tourism**: tourism directed toward exotic, often threatened, natural environments, intended to support conservation efforts and observe wildlife.
- **mass tourism**: tourism on a large scale to one country or region.
- **overrun by tourists**: invaded or occupied by a large number of tourists.
- **commercialized**: managed or exploited in a way designed to make a profit.
- **off the beaten track**: in or into an isolated place.
- **vibrant**: full of energy and enthusiasm.
- **cosmopolitan**: including or containing people from many different countries.
- **touts**: a person soliciting custom or business, typically in an aggressive or bold manner.
- **tacky**: showing poor taste and quality.
- **unspoilt**: (of a place) not marred by development.
- **breathtaking views**: extremely beautiful or amazing scenery.
- **remote and isolated**: situated far from the main centres of population.
- **cut off from civilization**: isolated from human society.

## 4. GRAMMAR & RESPONSE PATTERNS
- Pattern_1: "I'd like to talk about a country I've always wanted to visit, which is Malaysia." (Clear introduction for Part 2)
- Pattern_2: "One of the things that attracts me is it's such a diverse nation and I think there's something for everyone." (Explaining reasons using 'One of the things that... is...')

- Pattern_8: "Car drivers can be so selfish; they always try to overtake and rarely give you enough room..." (Using a semicolon to link two closely related independent clauses)

# TOPIC: Time management and history

## 1. PERSONA POOL
- [P1] **The Planner/Optimizer**: Obsessed with time management, avoiding procrastination, and maximizing efficiency. They talk about devoting time, reducing wasted time, and schedules.
- [P2] **The Historian**: Fascinated by bygone eras, ancient traditions, and preserving historical sites for subsequent generations. Values learning from the past.
- [P3] **The Futurist**: Always looking ahead. Interested in emerging technologies, predictions for the coming decade, and how modern society will evolve.
- [P4] **IELTS Examiner**: Professional, neutral examiner assessing fluency, vocabulary, and grammar.

## 2. QUESTION POOL
### Band 5.0 - 6.0
- Q_5_01: What is your favourite time of day? And your least favourite? Why?
- Q_5_02: At which time of day do you function most efficiently?
- Q_5_03: In which situations do you feel time moves too fast or too slowly?
- Q_5_04: Do you think time management is important? Why/Why not?
- Q_5_05: How well do you manage your time?

### Band 6.5+
- Q_7_01: How do you think the tourist industry will develop in the future?
- Q_7_02: To what extent do you think advertising affects the way people shop?
- Q_7_03: What effect can watching television have on children?
- Q_7_04: How do you think the leisure industry will develop in the coming years?
- Q_7_05: Do you think people should stay in the same jobs all their lives?
- Q_7_06: What will the situation be if the use of private cars continues to increase?

## 3. VOCABULARY & COLLOCATION POOL
### Band 5.0 - 6.0
- **waste time**: use time carelessly or to no purpose.
- **allow time**: to leave enough time to do something.
- **reduce time**: to make the amount of time less.
- **devote time**: to give all of or a large part of one's time to a particular activity.
- **spend time**: to pass time in a specified way.
- **recently**: at a recent time; not long ago.
- **nowadays**: at the present time, in contrast with the past.
- **plan**: decide on and arrange in advance.
- **practise**: perform (an activity) or exercise (a skill) repeatedly or regularly.
- **seldom**: not often; rarely.

### Band 6.5+
- **subsequent generations**: the generations that come after the present one.
- **ancient traditions**: a custom or belief that has existed for a very long time.
- **bygone times**: belonging to an earlier time.
- **contemporary society**: the society we live in today.
- **emerging technologies**: technologies whose development, practical applications, or both are still largely unrealized.
- **are inclined**: have a tendency to do something.
- **don't anticipate**: do not expect or predict.
- **forecast**: predict or estimate (a future event or trend).
- **in the coming decade**: in the next ten years.
- **near future**: very soon.
- **sooner or later**: eventually.
- **mass-produced**: produced in large quantities by an automated mechanical process.
- **far-reaching**: having important and widely applicable effects or implications.
- **cost-effective**: effective or productive in relation to its cost.
- **time-consuming**: taking a lot of or too much time.

## 4. GRAMMAR & RESPONSE PATTERNS
- Pattern_1: "It's highly likely that the tourist industry will..." (Speculating about the future with high probability)
- Pattern_2: "I doubt whether..." (Speculating about the future with low probability)

## 3. VOCABULARY & COLLOCATION POOL

### Band 5.0 - 6.0
- **greenhouse gases**: gases (like CO2) that trap heat in the atmosphere and cause global warming
- **energy efficiency**: using less energy to achieve the same result, e.g. efficient light bulbs
- **car-pooling**: several people sharing one car to commute together, reducing traffic
- **fuel consumption**: the amount of fuel used by a vehicle or process
- **waste paper collection**: the practice of gathering used paper so it can be recycled
- **recycled fibre**: material made from paper that has already been used once
- **single-occupant vehicle**: a car carrying only the driver, with no passengers
- **exhaust emission**: the gases released from a vehicle's engine
- **mass transit**: public transport systems (buses, trains) designed to move large numbers of people
- **fuel-efficient building code**: construction regulations designed to reduce energy waste in buildings

### Band 6.5+
- **phasing out (something)**: gradually stopping the use of something, e.g. "phasing out chlorofluorocarbons"
- **stiff resistance**: strong opposition to a plan or policy, e.g. "recommendations would meet with stiff resistance"
- **irreversible consequences**: effects/results that cannot be undone once they happen
- **sustainable resource**: a resource that can be replaced or renewed naturally, e.g. trees for paper
- **biodegradable**: able to decompose naturally without harming the environment
- **down-cycled**: recycled into a lower-quality product than the original material
- **non-renewable energy source**: an energy source (e.g. fossil fuel) that cannot be replaced once used
- **complementary policies**: policies designed to work together and reinforce each other (e.g. transport investment + car-use restrictions)
- **repulping process**: the industrial process of breaking waste paper back down into fibres for reuse
- **de-inked (fibre)**: paper fibre that has had printing ink chemically removed before reuse
- **contaminant (in recycling)**: unwanted material (e.g. staples, plastic) mixed into collected recyclables
- **potential for reducing (emissions)**: the capacity or scope for lowering pollution levels

## 4. GRAMMAR & RESPONSE PATTERNS

- Pattern_1 (Concession): "While [acknowledge one side], [main claim/counterpoint]." → e.g. "While paper recycling saves raw materials, it still requires fossil fuels to collect and process."
- Pattern_2 (Emphatic addition): "Not only do/does [clause A], but [clause B] as well/also." → e.g. "Not only do buses reduce fuel use per passenger, but they also cut harmful emissions."
- Pattern_3 (Passive prediction/estimation): "[Something] is expected/estimated to [verb] by/at [figure]." → e.g. "The use of recycled fibre is expected to increase at twice the rate of virgin fibre."
- Pattern_4 (Comparative statistic): "[A] requires/emits far less [resource] than [B] does, per [unit]." → e.g. "Trains require far less fuel per passenger than single-occupant cars do."
- Pattern_5 (Policy urging, modal + infinitive): "It urged [authority] to [action 1], [action 2], and [action 3]." → e.g. "It urged lawmakers to raise energy prices, improve building codes, and increase support for mass transit."
- Pattern_6 (Result clause after quantity change): "As [resource] becomes scarcer/cheaper, [consequence] sets in." → e.g. "As virgin fibre becomes more expensive, greater use of recycled paper sets in."
- Pattern_7 (Cautious optimism/doubt): "[Group] applauded [X], while worrying that [Y]." → e.g. "Environmentalists applauded the report, while worrying that its recommendations would meet resistance."
